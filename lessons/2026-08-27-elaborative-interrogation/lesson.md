## The concept

Elaborative interrogation is the simple habit of asking "why would that
be true?" or "why does that make sense?" about a new fact or claim, and
actually trying to answer it, instead of accepting it and moving on to
the next sentence. It sounds almost too simple to matter, but it changes
what happens to that fact in memory in a specific, well-supported way.

A fact encountered in isolation — read once, accepted, moved past — gets
stored as roughly one isolated point, with few paths leading back to it
later. Forcing yourself to explain *why* it's true requires connecting it
to things you already know, and every one of those connections becomes an
additional path that can later lead memory back to the original fact. A
fact with three or four "why" connections built to prior knowledge is far
more findable later than the same fact sitting alone with no links to
anything.

This is closely related to why teaching something to someone else is such
a strong learning method — explaining forces the exact same kind of
connective work elaborative interrogation does deliberately: you can't
explain *why* something is true without linking it to something the
listener (or you) already understands.

## Why it matters

This is, structurally, exactly what this project's own "Why it matters"
section is already doing in every single lesson — deliberately connecting
a new idea back to something already covered, or to this project itself,
rather than leaving it isolated. Worth treating as a real, load-bearing
design choice going forward, not just a nice closing paragraph.
