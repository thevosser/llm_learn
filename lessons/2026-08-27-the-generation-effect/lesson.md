## The concept

The generation effect is a specific, well-replicated finding: information
you attempt to produce yourself — even when the attempt is wrong — is
remembered better afterward than the same information simply handed to
you outright, as long as you eventually see the correct version. The act
of reaching for an answer, even unsuccessfully, appears to prime memory
in a way that passively receiving the same information never does.

This is a distinct idea from retrieval practice, not a restatement of it.
Retrieval practice is about testing what you've already learned, after
the fact, to strengthen it. The generation effect is about *ordering*: try
to produce the answer *before* you're told it, rather than reading the
explanation first and testing yourself later. A wrong guess, followed
immediately by the correct answer, tends to outperform being given the
correct answer with no attempt at all — the wrongness of the guess isn't
a failure to route around, it's part of what makes the eventual
correction stick.

This only works when the correction actually arrives, and reasonably
soon — a guess that never gets corrected just reinforces the wrong
answer. The mechanism is guess-then-correct, not guess-and-move-on.

## Why it matters

This is a concrete argument for how future lessons could be structured:
posing a question and inviting a real guess *before* the explanation,
rather than explaining first and testing afterward. The toggle widgets
already get partway there — you engage with an option before reading
its content — but a dedicated "guess, then reveal" format would push this
principle further and more deliberately than the current lesson format
does.
